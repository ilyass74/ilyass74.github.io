THE ENGINEERING QUESTION

An entrepreneurial tourism technology project, built for a more informed discovery of Morocco.

Morocco’s places carry cultural meaning that a list of attractions cannot explain. Travelers need trustworthy context, usable local information and help deciding what to explore. ANSUF is my independent entrepreneurial response: a tourism technology product combining geographic discovery, reviewed knowledge and intelligent assistance, starting with Marrakech. Its ambition is to create practical value for visitors and the Moroccan tourism ecosystem. That intended value is a product direction to validate, not a claim of measured economic impact or a commercial launch.

An entrepreneurial project with a Moroccan purpose

ANSUF is more than a technical demonstration: it is an independent product initiative I intend to develop into a useful tourism experience. The opportunity I am exploring is the gap between finding a place and understanding why it matters, what information is trustworthy and how to make an informed choice nearby. The first product scope is Marrakech. Extending it across Morocco would require reliable local knowledge, human review and an operating model that can maintain that knowledge over time. No nationwide availability, partnerships, customer adoption or revenue are claimed.

Who it is for, and what value it aims to create

The initial audience is visitors looking for cultural context and practical help while exploring, including people making decisions as a group. The intended local benefit is clearer, more accurate visibility for relevant places and experiences, supported by their identity, sources and geographic context. Cultural content should help a visitor understand a place rather than replace its meaning with generic AI prose. These are design objectives: visitor usefulness, local discoverability and cultural accuracy still need to be assessed through actual use and review.

My role: connect product decisions to the complete system

I lead the independent product direction and engineering: translating the tourism problem into user journeys, shaping the data and publication model, building the frontend and API, integrating geographic retrieval and bounded assistance, and organizing technical validation. The implementation uses a Next.js/TypeScript frontend with FastAPI/Python and PostgreSQL/PostGIS. A modular monolith keeps HTTP, domain rules, persistence and provider boundaries explicit. This product work is separate from my professional ERP collaboration with B Tel Bureautique.

Publication is part of the data architecture

City, neighborhood and place entities sit alongside source records and knowledge assertions. Origin and review state are separate concepts: a real-origin record is not automatically verified, and demo/test content cannot become verified content. A real validation requires evidence, a reviewer and a date. Retrieval checks source validity, geographic scope, freshness and parent publication. A reviewed place does not automatically validate every sentence about it. Private curation drafts remain separate from published knowledge.

A local knowledge pipeline needs review, not just collection

Later local development adds a private Scout curation workflow and an internal format for additive geographic releases. Candidate records, source snapshots and owner decisions are kept distinct from the live catalog. Deterministic plans, file fingerprints and typed evidence contracts make an intended addition inspectable before it becomes publishable. Geographic release work separates structural composition, source and authority review, and a controlled import recipe with replay and rollback boundaries. The inspected import implementation is restricted to disposable test environments; it does not establish a production content pipeline or approve the pending places.

Discover nearby places without hiding the location contract

Around Me combines explicit foreground location permission with a manual place-based alternative. PostGIS retrieval uses geographic distance and radius predicates; pure ranking works over already admitted projections. Location freshness and accuracy are checked before a recommendation is accepted. Distances describe geographic proximity, not a measured walking route. The later local Around Me work remains identifiable as working changes rather than being silently presented as the remote branch or a completed field release.

Rafiq and Rafika share one evidence boundary

The two companions share the same bounded assistance core; their identities are presentation metadata, not different factual authorities. Ask assembles admitted evidence and rechecks publication before returning an answer. An optional composer may select evidence identifiers but cannot grant publication approval or insert arbitrary factual prose. Deterministic fallback and explicit uncertainty preserve the boundary when composition fails. Language and voice capabilities have separate approval and rights requirements; an implemented contract does not imply approved recordings or live translation.

Private group decisions require more than a voting screen

Explore Together integrates guest identity, creator-approved invitations, a frozen cohort and complete ballots. A vote contains YES, MAYBE or SKIP for every frozen place. Group and round locks, expected revisions and database constraints serialize changes. The final completion computes the result and erases ballots within the same transaction. Withdrawal or expiry invalidates the whole cohort instead of quietly reducing the denominator. Shared APIs do not expose peer ballots or intermediate scores; this is API-level ballot confidentiality, not end-to-end encryption or mathematical anonymity.

Current maturity: built features, bounded validation

ANSUF has implemented product and engineering work across discovery, knowledge retrieval, companion assistance and private group decisions. The inspected private-preview sign-off is conditional and retains incomplete and blocked acceptance items. It is historical evidence for a specific candidate, not a fresh production audit. Later local curation and geographic-release branches are separate milestones from the remote development branch and the preview baseline. Public commercial launch, production scale and operational readiness are not established by these sources.

What a responsible launch needs next

The next product work is to validate the usefulness of the experience with visitors and local reviewers, resolve remaining physical-device and accessibility acceptance, and establish a sustainable process for checking and refreshing local information. Native-language content and recordings require their own review and rights. Operational preparation, content acceptance and a launch decision must remain explicit. The aim is a dependable product with a useful Moroccan contribution; commercial assumptions and wider geographic expansion should follow evidence rather than precede it.
